AI platforms, developers, corporations and marketplaces should verify applicable rights and licences before commercially distributing software, designs, documents or digital products. Publication through a marketplace does not itself prove permission or ownership.

## Reporting and investigation
1. Document the original work, allegedly copied material, relevant dates and evidence.
2. Check provenance, licences, assignments and permissions.
3. Distinguish allegations from verified findings; provide notice, fair review and an appeal route.
4. Preserve evidence securely and minimise personal data.
5. Refer credible concerns for human review. Do not automatically penalise, confiscate assets or accuse a person of theft.
6. Refer substantiated concerns to appropriate platform processes or qualified legal review.

## Owner control and ethics
Preserve original code, history, ownership and attribution. Require explicit owner approval before changes, publication, licensing, transfers or payments. Keep essential technical security mechanisms intact. Respect international human rights principles of dignity, equality, non-discrimination, privacy, due process, proportionality and freedom of expression, alongside applicable intellectual property and data protection law. Apply confidentiality and NDA terms only as legally valid; proposed penalties require legal review.

This is a policy proposal, not a finding of infringement or an authorisation for automated enforcement.
